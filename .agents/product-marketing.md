# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-31  
**Evidence state:** pre-PMF; current first-party interview corpus is synthetic and cannot validate demand.

## Product Overview

**Working name:** one-step-forward-agent  
**One-liner:** An evidence-backed work copilot that helps knowledge workers restore context, make explicit decisions, and compare actual time use with stated priorities.  
**Category hypothesis:** evidence-backed personal work copilot / decision-and-reflection agent.  
**Product type:** AI SaaS / personal productivity agent.  
**Business model hypothesis:** individual subscription first; team/enterprise only after a clear individual retention signal.

## Target Audience

**Beachhead ICP hypothesis:** knowledge workers with frequent context switching and high decision load: product managers, founders, consultants, engineering/team leads, researchers, and operators.  
**Primary situation:** the user has several concurrent initiatives, fragmented evidence across tools, and no reliable way to reconstruct why a task or decision is in its current state.  
**Primary buyer:** individual user during discovery; team buyer is not yet validated.

### Jobs to be Done

1. **Restore context:** when returning to an interrupted initiative, reconstruct what happened, why prior decisions were made, and what remains open.
2. **Make a decision with evidence:** when several plausible options compete, externalize criteria, compare evidence, and commit without endless re-analysis.
3. **Get objective time-use feedback:** when perceived productivity is unreliable, compare actual attention allocation with declared goals and priorities.

## Problems & Pains

- Information exists, but causal context is fragmented across chats, tasks, docs, calendar, and browser history.
- Generic AI can answer questions but may not expose the evidence chain behind an answer.
- Planning tools optimize schedules but do not necessarily explain why a decision is reasonable.
- Time trackers show activity, but activity alone does not explain whether time served the user's priorities.
- Reconstructing context manually creates repeated cognitive setup cost.

## Competitive Alternatives

### Direct/adjacent products
- **Motion:** strong at automatic task prioritization, scheduling, calendar and project execution; adjacent to the "what should I do next?" job.
- **Rize / RescueTime:** strong at automatic time tracking, focus metrics and reporting; adjacent to objective time feedback.
- **Limitless:** historically strong at persistent memory and conversational recall; after its acquisition by Meta it stopped selling the Pendant to new customers, making it an important category signal more than a stable direct benchmark.

### Status-quo alternatives
- notes + calendar + task manager + search;
- spreadsheets / pros-and-cons matrices;
- asking colleagues or generic LLMs;
- manual weekly review;
- doing nothing and re-reading source material when context is lost.

## Differentiation Hypothesis

**Core wedge:** connect three normally separate loops — **what happened → why choose this → where time actually went** — while preserving source traceability.

**Unique value hypothesis:** the product does not merely plan or track. It builds an inspectable evidence chain from activity and context to decisions and retrospective feedback.

**Product principles that support positioning:**
- source-linked outputs and data lineage;
- visible decision criteria;
- user control over recommendations;
- no hidden "productivity score" presented as objective truth;
- reversible suggestions rather than autonomous irreversible actions.

## Switching Dynamics (JTBD Four Forces)

**Push:** repeated context reconstruction, decision paralysis, subjective weekly reviews, tool fragmentation.  
**Pull:** one place to see evidence, rationale, next step, and actual allocation of attention.  
**Habit:** existing task/calendar/note workflows are familiar and already paid for.  
**Anxiety:** privacy, passive monitoring, hallucinated recommendations, integration effort, fear of another productivity dashboard.

## Objections

| Objection | Response hypothesis |
|---|---|
| "I do not want surveillance." | Show exactly what is captured; allow source-level controls and explicit deletion; avoid screenshot-style employee monitoring positioning. |
| "AI recommendations hallucinate." | Every recommendation links to evidence and exposes criteria/confidence; unsupported claims are visibly marked. |
| "I already have Notion/Calendar/ChatGPT." | Position around connecting evidence across those systems rather than replacing every system. |
| "Another productivity tool will create more work." | Measure setup/review burden; design for passive capture plus short review loops. |

## Anti-Persona

- users who only need a lightweight to-do list;
- organizations primarily seeking employee surveillance;
- buyers demanding a full project-management suite in v0.1;
- users unwilling to grant any data access required for context reconstruction.

## Customer Language Policy

The current corpus is synthetic, so no phrase may be labeled a real customer quote. Synthetic phrasing can be used only as a **message hypothesis** until repeated in real interviews/reviews.

### Message hypotheses to validate
- "Я трачу время не на работу, а на восстановление того, где остановился."
- "Я не понимаю, почему это решение казалось правильным неделю назад."
- "Календарь показывает встречи, но не показывает, куда на самом деле ушло внимание."

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** calm, precise, evidence-led.  
**Style:** explain the basis of a recommendation; avoid hustle culture and guilt.  
**Personality:** analytical, transparent, pragmatic, respectful of user agency.

## Proof State

There are currently **no empirical customer proof points** in this repository. The 30 interview files are synthetic fixtures. Until real discovery data is imported, positioning, ICP, pricing, and channel choices remain hypotheses.

## Goals

**Primary near-term goal:** validate whether one of the three JTBD has sufficient frequency, intensity, and willingness-to-change to support a beachhead product.  
**Primary conversion action:** recruit qualified prospects for problem interviews and concierge prototypes before optimizing paid acquisition.  
**North-star learning metric:** number of high-confidence problem signals tied to a specific segment and trigger, not raw traffic.
