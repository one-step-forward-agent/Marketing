# Competitive alternatives — current market scan

**Snapshot date:** 2026-08-31  
**Purpose:** understand the customer's alternatives and category expectations. This is not a feature-comparison sales page. Refresh before external use.

## Strategic frame

The most important competitor is often **status quo**: calendar + tasks + notes + generic LLM + manual weekly review. The product should not attempt to beat every adjacent product at its strongest job. The proposed wedge is an inspectable evidence chain across context, decisions and time allocation.

## Current alternatives

| Alternative | Current product emphasis | Strongest adjacent JTBD | What users may already expect | White-space hypothesis to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Motion | AI task planner, automatic prioritization/time-blocking, project/task aggregation | "what should I work on next?" | automatic planning that adapts when priorities/deadlines change | next-step guidance tied to explicit evidence/rationale, not schedule optimization alone |

## Key competitive observations

### Motion
Motion currently emphasizes automatic task prioritization, planning and dynamic calendar optimization. This validates strong category demand for "tell me what to do next" but does not by itself validate the need for source-linked causal context.

### Reclaim.ai
Reclaim spans tasks, focus time, habits, scheduling and stats. Its 2026 Reclaim 2.0 task documentation explicitly moves toward AI-assisted prioritization. This increases overlap with the prioritization job and makes **evidence-backed rationale** more important as differentiation.

### RescueTime / Rize
Both create expectations that objective work telemetry can be captured passively. Their strength raises the bar for the time-feedback JTBD: a new product needs to answer a different question than "which app did I use?". The hypothesis is **priority alignment and explanation**, not raw activity reporting.

### Mem
Mem's current positioning as an AI chief of staff with durable context indicates that persistent work memory is becoming a mainstream AI-agent promise. The differentiation cannot simply be "AI remembers"; it needs to be verifiable lineage, explicit decisions and user-controlled evidence.

## Positioning implication

Avoid an "all-in-one AI productivity app" claim. A more falsifiable wedge is:

```text
source evidence
   → reconstructed work context
   → explicit decision criteria/rationale
   → observed time allocation/outcome
   → next review
```

The differentiation hypothesis is **traceable continuity**, not more automation.

## Competitive research gaps

Before external positioning is finalized, collect:

- real switching stories from users of at least 2 adjacent alternatives;
- why users stop using time trackers/planners;
- which data permissions cause refusal;
- whether source provenance materially changes trust;
- willingness to add another tool vs. use an integration into existing stack;
- price/packaging anchors in the winning segment.
