# Pricing hypotheses v0.1

**Evidence status:** unvalidated. Pricing research must follow problem/value validation; no price in this file is a recommendation to charge today.

## Pricing principle

Price should map to the **validated unit of value**, not to token usage or number of AI messages unless customers perceive that as the value unit.

## Candidate packaging models

### A. Individual subscription
Best initial hypothesis if the validated loop is personal and recurrent.

Potential value anchors:
- hours of context reconstruction avoided;
- reduced repeated decision setup;
- recurring weekly review utility.

Main risk: the perceived pain is episodic, producing weak monthly retention.

### B. Prosumer / professional tier
For consultants, founders or leads with higher information load and multiple workstreams.

Potential differentiators:
- more connected sources;
- longer history;
- advanced lineage/export;
- decision history and review;
- higher automation limits.

Main risk: packaging features before a core habit is validated.

### C. Team / organization tier — deferred
Possible future value: shared decision lineage, project context and governance. Not a v0.1 target because collaboration, admin, security and procurement would dramatically increase scope.

## Willingness-to-pay research

Do not ask only "Would you pay X?". Prefer:

1. What do you use/pay for today to solve this job?
2. What is the consequence of not solving it?
3. What budget/category would this replace or complement?
4. Would you continue a concierge version after the free research period?
5. At what point would the tool be too cheap to trust / expensive to justify? (after value is experienced)

## Pricing evidence ladder

- **0 — assumption:** internal price guess.
- **1 — stated:** user says they might pay.
- **2 — commitment:** user agrees to a paid pilot / deposit / budget approval.
- **3 — transaction:** actual payment.
- **4 — retention:** repeated payment with continued usage.

Pricing decisions should not be treated as validated below level 2.

## Guardrails

- avoid unlimited high-cost LLM usage without unit economics;
- expose plan limits clearly;
- do not use dark patterns around trial cancellation;
- keep data export/delete independent of upsell pressure;
- enterprise pricing requires security/admin scope, not only a larger seat count.
